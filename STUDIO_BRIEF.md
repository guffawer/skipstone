## 5. Game Concept

### Core Loop
1. Pick a stone and a lake.
2. Swipe to throw: swipe speed sets power and swipe curvature sets spin. Tilting the phone sets the throw angle, shown as an on-screen arc.
3. The stone skips according to the physics model; skips and distance are scored.
4. Earn coins and XP, unlock and upgrade stones, unlock new lakes.
5. Enter the weekly tournament and climb the division leaderboard.

### Physics Model (approved concept; parameters tuned in Gate 2)
| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Throw angle | Optimal entry angle around 20 degrees to the water; steeper angles sink the stone, very shallow angles lose speed |
| Speed | More speed means more skips; each bounce loses energy |
| Spin | Stabilises the stone; spin decays per bounce |
| Stone stats | Shape, weight and flatness modify energy loss and stability |
| Conditions | Wind and water chop vary by lake and weather, generated by code |

### Progression (proposed, pending Gate 2 and Gate 4 approval)
| System | Detail |
|---|---|
| Stones | Collectible stones with stats (flatness, weight, spin retention) and rarity tiers; upgradeable with coins |
| Lakes | "World tour" of famous lakes as unlockable levels, each with its own conditions and look |
| Divisions | Novice, Amateur, Pro, Master, set by stone power and player level |
| Tournaments | Weekly asynchronous tournaments per division; in-game prizes only |
| Leaderboards | Google Play Games Services (Android) and Game Center (iOS); free, no custom servers |
| Daily challenge | One fixed throw condition per day with a shareable result card, to drive organic sharing |
| Aspirational hook | The real-world stone-skipping record (88 skips) as a long-term target |

### Content Without Ongoing Development
- Lake conditions, weather and daily challenges are generated by code.
- Seasonal themes (monsoon, winter and similar) are prepared once and scheduled.
- New lakes and stones can be added as data rather than new code.

## 13. Gate 2 Build Notes (prototype v0.1)

### Controls as built
| Control | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Swipe power | Peak finger speed over any 80 ms window of the swipe; a fast flick is full power, a slow drag is minimum power |
| Swipe spin | Sideways curve of the swipe path; a straight swipe gives no spin and a wobbling stone |
| Angle | Slider always available; tilt optional. The angle is locked at the moment the finger touches down, so tilting during the swipe does not change it |
| Tilt calibration | The phone pose held when tilt is switched on counts as 20 degrees; 1 degree of phone tilt moves the angle 0.6 degrees. Direction of tilt is untested |
| Result card | After each throw: skips, distance, and angle, power and spin with a plain verdict on each, so the player can see why the throw worked or failed |

### Starter stones (tuning values)
| Stone | Flatness | Mass | Spin retention | Drag per bounce | Trait |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| River Pebble | 0.60 | 0.50 | 0.94 | 5.0% | Balanced |
| Flat Slate | 0.95 | 0.30 | 0.96 | 9.0% | Most skips; touchy to steep angles |
| Granite Disc | 0.30 | 1.10 | 0.90 | 2.4% | Fewer skips; forgiving of angle and wobble |

### Model results from 60-throw simulations (calm wind)
| Throw | River Pebble | Flat Slate | Granite Disc |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strong, 15 deg, full spin | 9 skips, 78 m | 16 skips, 100 m | 6 skips, 47 m |
| Good, 15 deg, 80% power, 60% spin | 5 skips, 34 m | 8 skips, 47 m | 5 skips, 28 m |
| Steep, 35 deg | 3 skips, 11 m | 3 skips, 7 m | 5 skips, 19 m |
| No spin | 2 skips, 14 m | 3 skips, 17 m | 3 skips, 18 m |
